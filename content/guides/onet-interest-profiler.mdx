The O\*NET Interest Profiler is a free career interest test from the U.S. Department of Labor. It asks you how much you would enjoy 60 different work activities and uses your answers to measure your interest in six areas of work. Those six areas, known as RIASEC, connect directly to the careers in the O\*NET database.

This guide explains who made it, how it works, how it is scored, and how to turn your results into a shortlist of careers.

## Who made it

The Interest Profiler was developed for the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration, by the O\*NET program, the same program that maintains the official database of U.S. occupations. It is based on John Holland's theory of vocational interests, the RIASEC model, and is used by schools, workforce centers and career counselors across the country.

The Department of Labor offers it through its own site, My Next Move. The question items are also available to other sites under a Creative Commons license (CC BY-ND 4.0), which requires that they be used without changes. Our free [career test](/career-test) uses the 60 items of the official Short Form unchanged.

## What the questions look like

Each item is a short work activity, such as building kitchen cabinets, developing a new medicine, writing books or plays, teaching a high-school class, selling merchandise at a department store, or keeping shipping and receiving records. For each one you choose an answer on a five-point scale:

- Strongly Dislike
- Dislike
- Unsure
- Like
- Strongly Like

The instructions ask you not to think about whether you have the education or training for the activity, or how much money it pays, only whether you would enjoy it.

## How it is scored

The 60 items are divided evenly across the six interest areas, 10 items each. Every answer earns from 0 points (Strongly Dislike) to 4 points (Strongly Like), so each area gets a score from 0 to 40.

The six areas are:

| Area | What it is about |
|---|---|
| Realistic | Hands-on work with tools, machines, plants, animals |
| Investigative | Research, analysis, science and math |
| Artistic | Creating, designing, writing, performing |
| Social | Helping, teaching, caring for people |
| Enterprising | Leading, persuading, selling |
| Conventional | Organizing data, records and procedures |

Your highest areas form your interest profile, often written as a two- or three-letter code. Our guide to the [Holland Code and the RIASEC types](/guides/holland-code-riasec-explained) explains each one in depth.

If you answer "Strongly Dislike" to everything, every area scores zero, and the official message suggests retaking the Interest Profiler at a different time, because the results may not reflect your interests.

## How results connect to careers

Every occupation in O\*NET has its own interest profile, rated on the same six areas. That makes it possible to compare your profile with each career's. Careers whose main interest areas match your highest scores are likely to involve work you enjoy.

On this site, the career test results rank careers by how closely their interest profiles follow the same pattern as yours (the method described in the official manual), and add pay from the Bureau of Labor Statistics and the preparation each career needs. The test also adds 13 questions about the workday you want, such as how much contact with people or physical work you prefer, to sort the matches further.

## How accurate is it?

The Interest Profiler measures interests, not ability or personality. It is a well-researched tool for exploring options, and it is good at telling you which kinds of work you would likely enjoy. It does not tell you:

- whether you would be good at a job;
- whether a job pays enough or is available where you live;
- whether you would like the people, schedule or setting.

Use the results as a starting list, then research each career's tasks, pay and training. Our guide on [how to research a career](/guides/how-to-research-a-career) shows how.

## Tips for useful results

1. **Answer for yourself, not for your current job.** Think about what you would enjoy, not what you are used to.
2. **Ignore training and pay.** You will check those later.
3. **Use the full scale.** If everything is "Like", the results cannot separate your interests.
4. **Answer quickly.** Your first reaction is usually the most accurate.
5. **Retake it after a few years** if your situation has changed a lot.

## Interest Profiler vs. other career tests

- **Personality tests** such as the Big Five measure how you tend to behave, not what work you enjoy. They complement an interest test. Try our free [Big Five personality test](/big-five-personality-test).
- **Skills-based tools** start from what you can already do. If you have work experience, the [career change tool](/career-change/tool) matches your current job's skills to other careers.
- **Commercial tests** often add longer reports. Our page on [career test alternatives](/alternatives) compares several.

## Example: reading a result

Suppose your six scores out of 40 are:

| Area | Score |
|---|---|
| Social | 34 |
| Artistic | 29 |
| Investigative | 21 |
| Enterprising | 15 |
| Conventional | 9 |
| Realistic | 6 |

Your interest code would be **SAI**: mainly Social, then Artistic, then Investigative. That points toward careers that help people in creative or thoughtful ways, such as teaching, counseling, or art and music therapy, rather than hands-on technical work, where your Realistic score is low.

Look at the gaps between scores too. A clear lead, like Social here, is a strong signal. If your top three are within a few points of each other, your interests are broad, and careers that mix those areas may suit you best.

## Common questions

### Is the O\*NET Interest Profiler free?

Yes. It is published by the U.S. Department of Labor and is free to take, both on My Next Move and on sites that use the official items, such as our [career test](/career-test).

### How many questions are on the O\*NET Interest Profiler?

The standard version has 60 questions, 10 for each of the six interest areas. It usually takes about 10 minutes.

### What is a good score on the Interest Profiler?

There are no good or bad scores. Each area is scored from 0 to 40, and what matters is which areas are highest for you compared with your other areas.

### Can I use the Interest Profiler to change careers?

Yes. Compare your top interest areas with your current job's profile, then look for careers that fit your interests and use skills you already have. The [career change tool](/career-change/tool) shows how close each career is to your current job.
