When you change careers, your LinkedIn profile still tells your old story. Recruiters searching for the new role will not find you, and people who do find you will not understand where you are going. A few changes fix that: a new headline, an About section that explains the move, and job descriptions that highlight transferable results.

## Start with the headline

The headline is the most important line on your profile. It appears in search results, comments and invitations. Use it to name where you are going, not only where you have been.

### Headline formulas

- **Target role + background:** "Project Coordinator | 8 Years Leading Retail Operations"
- **Target role + key skill:** "Corporate Trainer | Curriculum Design and Adult Learning"
- **Transition statement:** "Former Teacher Moving Into Instructional Design | eLearning, Storyline, Curriculum"
- **Credential + target:** "Certified Medical Coder (CPC) | Former Medical Office Manager"

Use the words recruiters search for in your new field. Read 15 to 20 job ads for the role you want and borrow the most common terms.

## Write an About section that tells the story

Your About section answers the question every reader has: why the change, and why you? Use three short paragraphs.

1. **Where you are going.** One or two sentences naming the new role or field.
2. **What you bring.** Your strongest transferable skills, with one or two specific results.
3. **What you have done to prepare.** Courses, certificates, projects or volunteer work in the new field.

### Template

> I'm a [new role] with a background in [old field]. I'm moving into [new field] because [specific reason].
>
> In [number] years as a [old role], I [result that uses a skill the new job needs] and [second result]. I'm known for [strength].
>
> To prepare, I [course/certificate/project]. I'm looking for [type of role] where I can [what you want to do].

### Example

> I'm a project coordinator with a background in retail operations. I'm moving into project management because the store remodels I ran were the most rewarding part of my work.
>
> In eight years as a store manager, I coordinated four remodels on time and within budget, scheduled teams of up to 25 people and cut inventory losses by 18%.
>
> This year I earned the CAPM certificate and coordinated a volunteer renovation project for a local food bank. I'm looking for a coordinator role in construction or facilities.

## Rewrite your past jobs

Do not delete your past career. It is where your skills came from. Instead, rewrite the descriptions so they highlight what matters in the new field.

- **Lead with transferable results.** Put the bullet points most relevant to the new career first.
- **Translate jargon.** Replace field-specific terms with words the new field uses. "Differentiated instruction" becomes "adapted training for different learning needs."
- **Use numbers.** Team sizes, budgets, time saved and customers served show scale to any reader.

To find which skills transfer, compare your current job with your target on this site. Every [career profile](/careers) lists its most important skills from O\*NET, and the [career change tool](/career-change/tool) shows how closely two careers match.

## Add proof of the new direction

- **Licenses and certifications:** list new credentials, even if still in progress ("expected May 2027").
- **Courses:** add relevant courses under Education or Licenses & certifications.
- **Projects:** use the Projects section, or the Featured section, for work samples such as a design portfolio or a training plan.
- **Volunteer experience:** volunteer roles in the new field count. List them with results.

## Update your skills section

LinkedIn lets you list skills and pin your top ones. Put the skills from your target career first. Remove skills that are only relevant to your old field so they do not crowd out the new ones.

## Turn on "Open to Work" carefully

You can show recruiters that you are open to new roles, with job titles and locations. If you are still employed, choose the option visible only to recruiters. List the new job titles you want, not your current one.

## Tell your network

A short post about your change can lead to introductions and openings. Keep it positive and specific:

> After 10 years in teaching, I'm moving into instructional design. I've completed a certificate in eLearning design and built my first course for a local nonprofit. I'd love to connect with people working in L&D or instructional design, and I'm happy to share what I learned along the way.

Then follow up with people who respond. Many will be happy to talk; see our guide to [informational interviews](/guides/informational-interviews).

## A quick LinkedIn checklist

- Headline names the new role and your strongest relevant skill.
- About section explains the move in three short paragraphs.
- Past jobs lead with transferable results and use the new field's words.
- New certificates, courses and projects are listed.
- Top skills match the target career.
- Open to Work lists the new job titles.
- A short post tells your network what you are looking for.

## Common questions

### Should I remove my old job from LinkedIn?

No. Your past jobs show your experience and where your skills came from. Rewrite the descriptions to highlight transferable results instead of removing them.

### What should my LinkedIn headline say during a career change?

Lead with the role you want and add your background or key skill: for example, "Project Coordinator | 8 Years Leading Retail Operations". Use the terms recruiters search for in the new field.

### Should I announce my career change on LinkedIn?

Yes, once you are committed. A short, positive post often leads to useful contacts. If you are still employed and the change is not public, update your profile quietly first.

### How do I show a career change if I have no experience in the new field?

Show preparation: courses, certificates, volunteer work and small projects. Then connect your past results to the new field's main tasks in your About section.
