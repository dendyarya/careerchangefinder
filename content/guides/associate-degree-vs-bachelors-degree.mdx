An associate degree takes about two years and costs much less than a bachelor's degree, which usually takes four. On average, bachelor's degree holders earn more. But some associate degree careers pay more than many jobs that need a bachelor's, and the associate route lets you start earning two years sooner.

This guide compares the two on time, cost, pay and career options, so you can decide which fits your goals.

## The two degrees at a glance

| | Associate degree | Bachelor's degree |
|---|---|---|
| Typical length (full time) | About 2 years | About 4 years |
| Where | Community colleges, technical colleges, some universities | Colleges and universities |
| Average in-state tuition and fees (public, 2022–23) | About $4,000 a year | About $9,800 a year |
| Median weekly earnings (2025) | $1,135 | $1,578 |
| Unemployment rate (2025) | 3.0% | 2.8% |

Sources: tuition, [NCES Fast Facts](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=76) (in-state, public institutions); earnings and unemployment, [BLS, Education pays, 2025](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2026/data-on-display/education-pays.htm) (full-time workers age 25 and older).

On average, bachelor's degree holders earned $443 more per week in 2025, about 39% more than associate degree holders. That gap is real, but it hides big differences by field.

## Careers where an associate degree is the usual path

O\*NET asks workers what education their job needs. For 26 careers with their own BLS pay figure, the most common answer is an associate degree. Their median pay is **$70,340**, well above the U.S. median for all workers of $50,980 (BLS, May 2025). The highest-paid among them:

| Career | Median pay |
|---|---|
| [Radiation therapists](/careers/radiation-therapists) | $105,310 |
| [Nuclear medicine technologists](/careers/nuclear-medicine-technologists) | $101,370 |
| [Dental hygienists](/careers/dental-hygienists) | $98,100 |
| [MRI technologists](/careers/magnetic-resonance-imaging-technologists) | $95,480 |
| [Cardiovascular technologists and technicians](/careers/cardiovascular-technologists-and-technicians) | $74,310 |
| [Electrical and electronics repairers](/careers/electrical-and-electronics-repairers) | $74,090 |
| [Occupational therapy assistants](/careers/occupational-therapy-assistants) | $72,300 |
| [Physical therapist assistants](/careers/physical-therapist-assistants) | $68,380 |

Source: O\*NET 31.0 (most common education); BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025.

Most are in healthcare and technical fields, where a focused two-year program plus a license leads directly to a job.

## Pay across careers by education level

We also grouped the 610 careers with their own BLS pay figure and an O\*NET education profile by the education most workers report:

| Most common education in the career | Careers | Median of their median pay |
|---|---|---|
| High school diploma or less | 296 | $48,075 |
| Certificate, some college or associate degree | 76 | $63,810 |
| Bachelor's degree | 135 | $80,390 |
| Master's degree or higher | 103 | $99,080 |

The bachelor's group is higher on average, but the ranges overlap: a dental hygienist with an associate degree earns more than the typical worker in many bachelor's-level careers.

## When an associate degree is the better choice

- **You know the career you want** and it is one of the associate-level careers, especially in healthcare or technical fields.
- **You want to start earning sooner.** Two extra years of work can add up to a large head start.
- **Cost is a major concern.** Community college tuition is far lower.
- **You want a lower-risk start.** Many people complete an associate degree, work for a while, and then transfer credits to a bachelor's program, sometimes with employer support.

## When a bachelor's degree is the better choice

- **The career requires it,** as with teaching, engineering, accounting, most nursing management roles, and many business and science careers.
- **You want the widest range of options.** Many professional and management roles list a bachelor's as the minimum.
- **You plan graduate study,** which requires a bachelor's first.
- **You are undecided** and want time to explore before choosing a field.

## The transfer route: both, step by step

Many students start with an associate degree and then transfer to a four-year school. To make this work:

1. Choose an associate program designed for transfer (often called an associate of arts or associate of science).
2. Check transfer agreements between your community college and the universities you are considering.
3. Meet with an advisor early to plan courses that count toward your bachelor's major.

Done well, this route can cut the total cost of a bachelor's degree significantly.

## Questions to ask before you enroll

Whichever degree you choose, ask the program these questions first:

1. **Is the program accredited?** For healthcare careers, programmatic accreditation is often required to sit for the license exam.
2. **What share of graduates pass the license exam?** Programs usually publish this.
3. **Where do graduates work, and how quickly are they hired?**
4. **What is the total cost,** including fees, books, uniforms and clinical or lab costs?
5. **Do credits transfer** to four-year schools you might attend later?
6. **Are there part-time, evening or online options** if you need to keep working?
7. **Is there a waitlist?** Popular healthcare programs can have long waits or competitive entry.

Answers to these questions often matter more than the type of degree. A well-run associate program with strong licensing results can lead to better pay than a bachelor's degree in a field with few jobs.

## Common questions

### Is an associate degree worth it?

Often, yes, especially in fields where it leads directly to a licensed job. The 26 careers where an associate degree is the most common education and BLS publishes their own pay have a median of $70,340.

### How much more do bachelor's degree holders earn?

In 2025, median weekly earnings were $1,578 for bachelor's degree holders and $1,135 for associate degree holders, a difference of $443 a week, or about 39%, according to the BLS.

### What is the highest-paying job with an associate degree?

Among careers where an associate degree is the most common education, radiation therapists have the highest median pay at $105,310, followed by nuclear medicine technologists at $101,370 (BLS, May 2025).

### Can I get a bachelor's degree after an associate degree?

Yes. Many students transfer their associate credits to a four-year program and finish a bachelor's in about two more years of full-time study.
