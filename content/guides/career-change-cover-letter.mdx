When you change careers, your resume shows what you have done. Your cover letter explains why it fits a job you have never held. It is your chance to answer the reader's first question — "why is this person applying here?" — before they have to ask it.

This guide gives a four-part structure, a fill-in template and two complete examples.

## What a career change cover letter must do

A hiring manager reading your letter wants to know three things:

1. **Why you are switching,** in one or two sentences, framed as a move toward something.
2. **Why you can do this job,** with two or three examples that match what the role needs.
3. **What you have done to prepare,** such as a course, certificate, project or volunteer work.

Everything else is optional. Keep the letter to about 250 to 400 words, which fits on one page.

## The four-part structure

### 1. Opening: the role and the bridge

Name the job and give the one-sentence version of your story: where you are coming from, where you are going, and the link between them.

> I am applying for the Training Specialist role at Northwind Health. After nine years of designing and teaching lessons for groups of 25 to 30 students, I am moving into corporate training, where I can use the same skills with adult learners.

Avoid opening with an apology ("Although I have no experience in…"). Lead with what you bring.

### 2. Proof: two or three matching examples

Pick the two or three requirements that matter most in the posting and give one short example for each. Use the new field's vocabulary.

To find what matters most, check the career's profile on this site: it lists the skills O\*NET rates as most important for that job. For [Training and Development Specialists](/careers/training-and-development-specialists), instructing, social perceptiveness, judgment and coordination are rated highest, so a teacher should lead with examples of those.

> In my current role I assess what each learner needs, design material to close the gaps, and measure the results. Last year I rebuilt our science unit around short assessments, which helped me adjust lessons weekly. I also coached three new teachers through their first year, including observations and feedback.

### 3. Preparation: show you have already started

This paragraph separates a serious career changer from someone who is only curious.

> To prepare for this move, I completed a certificate in instructional design in 2026 and built a short onboarding course for new volunteers at a local nonprofit, which is now in use.

If you have not started yet, take one short course or do one small project before you apply. Every career profile on this site lists the usual education and any certification that is often expected, so you can see what employers look for.

### 4. Close: interest and a clear ask

Say why this employer, then ask for the next step.

> Northwind's focus on clinical staff development is exactly the kind of training I want to do. I would welcome the chance to discuss how my experience can help your team. Thank you for your time.

## Fill-in template

> Dear [Hiring Manager's name],
>
> I am applying for the [job title] role at [company]. After [number] years as a [current role], where I [one key thing you do that the new role also needs], I am moving into [new field] to [what you want to do there].
>
> In my current role, I [example 1 that matches requirement 1, with a result]. I also [example 2 that matches requirement 2, with a result]. These are the skills your posting puts first, and I use them every day.
>
> To prepare for this move, I [course, certificate, project or volunteer work, with the year].
>
> I am drawn to [company] because [one specific reason]. I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help [team or goal]. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your name]

## Example: retail to banking

> Dear Ms. Patel,
>
> I am applying for the Teller position at Lakeside Credit Union. After six years in retail, where I handled cash and helped customers every shift, I want to bring the same accuracy and service to banking.
>
> At my current store I balance a cash drawer at the end of every shift and process returns, exchanges and payments under store policy. I am the person managers call when a customer is upset, because I listen first and find a fix within the rules. I also train new cashiers on our point-of-sale system.
>
> This spring I completed an online course in banking basics and fraud awareness.
>
> Lakeside's focus on its local community matches why I enjoy customer work. I would welcome the chance to discuss the role. Thank you for your time.
>
> Sincerely,
> Alex Rivera

Why this works: in our data, [Tellers](/careers/tellers) are the closest match for [retail workers](/career-change/from-retail-worker), with a 93% skill match, and the letter shows the cash handling and service skills that make that match real.

## Example: nurse to clinical research

> Dear Dr. Morgan,
>
> I am applying for the Clinical Research Coordinator role at Riverside Medical Center. As a registered nurse for eight years, I have followed strict protocols, documented care in detail and explained treatment to patients and families. I am now moving into clinical research to work on how new treatments are tested.
>
> On my unit I document care for five to six patients per shift, and I served on our hospital's protocol review group, checking that our practice matched updated guidelines. Patients often tell me I make complex information easy to follow, which is central to informed consent.
>
> I recently completed Good Clinical Practice training and shadowed a research nurse on two studies.
>
> Riverside's oncology trials are an area I care about after years on an oncology floor. I would value the chance to discuss how my clinical experience can support your studies.
>
> Sincerely,
> Sam Okafor

Our [jobs for former nurses](/career-change/from-nurse) page lists clinical research coordination among the closest matches for nurses, based on O\*NET skills, knowledge and work activities.

All names and organizations in these examples are fictional.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Apologizing for your background.** Phrases like "Although I lack experience" put the gap first. Lead with the overlap.
- **Repeating your resume.** The letter should connect the dots, not list jobs.
- **Explaining why you dislike your current job.** Talk about what you are moving toward.
- **One letter for every application.** Change at least the opening, the examples and the closing for each role.
- **Vague claims.** "I am a strong communicator" proves nothing; one concrete example does.

## A quick checklist

- The first two sentences name the role and explain the switch.
- Each example matches a requirement in the posting.
- You mention at least one step you have taken to prepare.
- The letter is under one page and addressed to a person where possible.
- You ask for a conversation at the end.
