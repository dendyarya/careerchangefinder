A minor is a smaller set of courses, usually five to seven, in a second subject. It will not get you a job on its own, but the right one can make your major more useful, open a second set of careers, or simply add a subject you enjoy. This guide explains how to choose one with your career in mind.

## What a minor can and cannot do

**A minor can:**

- add a practical skill your major lacks, such as statistics, writing or programming;
- show employers an interest in a second field;
- connect your major to a specific industry;
- give you a backup direction if you change your mind.

**A minor cannot:**

- qualify you for a licensed career, such as nursing, teaching or accounting, on its own;
- replace work experience or internships;
- matter much to employers if it is unrelated to the job.

## Three ways to choose

### 1. The skills strategy: fill a gap in your major

Pick a minor that adds a skill employers ask for and your major does not teach. This is often the most valuable choice.

| If your major is… | Consider a minor in… | Because it adds… |
|---|---|---|
| Psychology, sociology, political science | Statistics or data analysis | Quantitative skills for research and analyst roles |
| English, history, communication | Business or marketing | A direct link to business careers |
| Biology, chemistry | Computer science or statistics | Data skills for research and health informatics |
| Computer science | Business or a field you want to work in (health, finance) | Industry knowledge |
| Art, design | Business or marketing | Skills to work with clients or run your own practice |
| Business | A language or computer science | International or technical options |

### 2. The industry strategy: point your major at a field

If you know the industry you want, a minor can connect your major to it. A communication major with a health sciences minor is a better fit for hospital communications; an economics major with an environmental studies minor fits energy and policy roles.

### 3. The interest strategy: study something you love

If your major already leads directly to a career, such as nursing or engineering, a minor in something you enjoy, like music or a language, can make college more rewarding. That is a valid choice. Just do not expect it to change your career prospects much.

## Why skill-based minors work

Some majors connect to many careers, and others connect to only a few. In the official crosswalk between college programs and occupations, the typical major is linked to just 3 careers with full data, and about 1 in 5 majors (20.8%) is linked to a single career. Quantitative and computing majors are linked to many more: statistics to 11, mathematics to 12 and computer science to 25.

A minor in one of these broad, skill-based fields borrows some of that flexibility. For more on which majors connect to the most careers, see [the most flexible college majors](/guides/most-flexible-college-majors).

## Questions to ask before you declare

1. **Which careers am I aiming for?** Look at the career profiles and note the skills and knowledge they rate as most important. The [college major quiz](/college-major-quiz) can suggest careers that fit your interests.
2. **Does the minor add a skill those careers use?** If yes, it is likely worth it.
3. **How many extra courses will it take?** Some minors overlap with general education or major requirements, so they cost little extra time.
4. **Will it delay graduation?** An extra semester costs tuition and income. A minor is rarely worth that delay.
5. **Can I get the same benefit another way?** A certificate, an internship or a few elective courses can sometimes add the same skill with less commitment.

## Minor vs. double major

| | Minor | Double major |
|---|---|---|
| Courses | About 5 to 7 | A full second set of major requirements |
| Time cost | Usually fits in four years | Often needs extra credits or semesters |
| Signal to employers | Added skill or interest | Deep training in two fields |
| Best for | Adding one useful skill | Students who need two full qualifications |

For most students, a well-chosen minor gives most of the career benefit of a double major with far less extra work.

## Common major and minor pairs

| Pair | Careers it can point toward |
|---|---|
| Psychology + statistics | Research assistant, market research analyst, data analyst |
| English + marketing | Content writing, communications, public relations |
| Biology + computer science | Bioinformatics, health data, laboratory software |
| Economics + computer science | Data analysis, financial technology |
| Communication + business | Sales, marketing, corporate communications |
| Political science + economics | Policy analysis, government, consulting |
| Nursing + a language | Patient care in bilingual communities |
| Art + business | Freelance design, gallery and arts management |

These are starting points, not guarantees. Check the career profiles for the roles you are interested in to see which skills and knowledge they value, and choose the minor that adds the most of them.

## A quick decision checklist

- The minor adds a skill or knowledge area my target careers use.
- It fits within my four-year plan without extra semesters.
- I enjoy the subject enough to do well in it.
- I could not get the same benefit more cheaply through a certificate or electives.

If you can tick all four, the minor is likely a good investment.

## Common questions

### Do employers care about minors?

Employers care when the minor adds a skill that matters for the job, such as statistics for an analyst role or a language for an international one. Unrelated minors rarely affect hiring decisions.

### What is the most useful minor?

For many majors, statistics, computer science, business or a writing-focused minor adds the broadest skills. The best choice depends on the careers you are aiming for and the gaps in your major.

### Is a minor worth it if it delays graduation?

Usually not. An extra semester costs tuition and income. Choose a minor that fits within your planned schedule, or add the skill through electives or a certificate instead.

### Can I add a minor later?

Yes, at most colleges you can declare a minor until your junior or senior year. Check your school's deadline and count how many courses you have already taken in the subject.
