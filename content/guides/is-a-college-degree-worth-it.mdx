For most people, a college degree still pays off: workers with a bachelor's degree earn far more and are less likely to be unemployed than workers with only a high school diploma. But "on average" hides big differences. The value of a degree depends on the field, the cost, whether you finish, and what you would do instead.

This guide shows the national numbers first, then the pay of specific careers by education level, and ends with a simple way to decide for yourself.

## Earnings and unemployment by education

The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes median weekly earnings and unemployment rates for workers age 25 and older by their highest level of education:

| Highest education | Median weekly earnings | Unemployment rate |
|---|---|---|
| Doctoral degree | $2,307 | 1.8% |
| Professional degree | $2,294 | 1.9% |
| Master's degree | $1,876 | 2.6% |
| Bachelor's degree | $1,578 | 2.8% |
| Associate's degree | $1,135 | 3.0% |
| Some college, no degree | $1,062 | 3.8% |
| High school diploma | $966 | 4.3% |
| Less than a high school diploma | $770 | 6.1% |
| All workers | $1,268 | 3.4% |

Source: [BLS, Education pays, 2025](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2026/data-on-display/education-pays.htm). Full-time wage and salary workers; 11-month averages because October 2025 data were not collected.

A bachelor's degree holder's median weekly pay, $1,578, is about 63% higher than a high school graduate's, $966. Over a 40-year career, that gap adds up to a large amount, before you subtract the cost of the degree.

Notice one more line: "Some college, no degree" earns only a little more than a high school diploma. Starting a degree and not finishing it costs money and time without most of the benefit.

## Pay by the education careers usually need

The national averages mix every field together. Looking at careers gives a clearer picture. We grouped the 610 careers on this site that have their own BLS pay figure and an O\*NET education profile by the education most workers in each career report:

| Most common education in the career | Careers | Median of their median pay |
|---|---|---|
| High school diploma or less | 296 | $48,075 |
| Certificate, some college or associate degree | 76 | $63,810 |
| Bachelor's degree | 135 | $80,390 |
| Master's degree or higher | 103 | $99,080 |

Source: O\*NET 31.0 education data and BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025; our calculation.

Each step up in education is linked to higher typical pay. But the ranges overlap a lot. Some careers that need no degree pay more than many that do; see [high-paying jobs without a degree](/careers/salary/high-paying-no-degree).

## When a degree is clearly worth it

- **The career requires it.** Nurses, teachers, engineers, accountants seeking a CPA license, physicians and lawyers cannot work without a specific degree.
- **The field pays well and hires steadily.** Degrees in healthcare, engineering, computing, accounting and many sciences lead to careers with high medians.
- **You are likely to finish.** Completion matters as much as enrollment.
- **The cost is reasonable.** In 2022–23, average tuition and fees were about $9,800 a year at public 4-year schools and about $4,000 at public 2-year colleges, for in-state students ([NCES Fast Facts](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=76)). Private schools and out-of-state tuition cost much more.

## When it may not be

- **You are unsure what to study** and would borrow heavily while deciding.
- **The career you want hires on skills** or certifications rather than degrees, which is common in many trades, sales and some technology roles.
- **A shorter program leads to similar pay.** Some certificate and associate degree careers pay above the national median; see our guide to [associate vs. bachelor's degrees](/guides/associate-degree-vs-bachelors-degree).
- **The debt would be large** compared with the likely pay in your chosen field.

## A simple way to decide

1. **Pick two or three target careers,** not just a degree. The [college major quiz](/college-major-quiz) and the [career test](/career-test) help you find them.
2. **Check what each one requires.** Each career profile shows the education most workers have and the usual preparation.
3. **Compare pay with and without the degree.** Use the median pay of your target careers against your realistic alternative.
4. **Estimate the full cost:** tuition, fees and the income you give up while studying.
5. **Divide cost by the yearly pay gain.** The result is roughly how many years it takes to break even. Under ten years usually means the degree is a good investment.

## What about adults going back to college?

The same logic applies, but the cost of lost income is higher for someone already working. For career changers, a degree is worth it mainly when the new career requires one. For most moves to similar careers, it is not needed. See [should you go back to school to change careers?](/guides/go-back-to-school-to-change-careers)

## A worked example

Imagine choosing between starting work after high school and a four-year degree that leads to a career with the median pay of the bachelor's group above.

- **Pay without the degree:** about $48,075 a year, the median of careers where most workers have a high school diploma or less.
- **Pay with the degree:** about $80,390 a year, the median of careers where most workers have a bachelor's degree.
- **Yearly gain:** about $32,315.
- **Cost:** four years of public in-state tuition and fees at about $9,800 a year is $39,200. Add four years of lost earnings at $48,075, or $192,300, for a total near $231,500.
- **Break-even:** about 7 years after starting work.

These are rough figures that ignore taxes, raises, scholarships and room and board, but the logic works for any choice. The higher the pay gain in your chosen field and the lower the cost, the faster a degree pays for itself.

## Common questions

### Do college graduates earn more?

Yes, on average. In 2025, the median weekly earnings of full-time workers with a bachelor's degree were $1,578, compared with $966 for those with only a high school diploma, according to the BLS.

### Is college worth it if I don't know what to study?

It can be, but plan carefully. Start at a lower-cost school, take a range of courses, and use a [major quiz](/college-major-quiz) to narrow your interests. Our guide on [how to choose a major](/guides/how-to-choose-a-major) can help.

### What are good careers without a degree?

Many trades, transportation and healthcare support careers pay well without a bachelor's degree. Browse [high-paying jobs without a degree](/careers/salary/high-paying-no-degree) and [careers by education level](/careers/education/no-degree).

### Does the major matter more than the degree?

Often, yes. The field you study shapes which careers you can enter and what they pay. Read [does your major matter?](/guides/does-your-major-matter)
