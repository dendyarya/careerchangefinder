The best major for you is one that leads to work you will actually enjoy. The most reliable way to find it is to start from your interests, then look at the majors whose careers match them.

This guide shows which majors fit each of the six interest types used by career counselors (the Holland Code, or RIASEC), based on U.S. government data. If you do not know your type yet, take the [College Major Quiz](/college-major-quiz) first: it uses the official O\*NET Interest Profiler questions and ranks majors for you in about 12 minutes.

## How we matched majors to interests

- The U.S. Department of Education links each college major (CIP code) to the careers it prepares students for.
- O\*NET rates every career on the six interest types, on a scale from 1 to 7.
- We gave each major the average interest profile of the careers it is linked to, and kept the 622 majors linked to at least five careers with full data, so one unusual career cannot decide the result.

The lists below show the majors with the highest score for each type. A score near 7 means the careers the major leads to are very strongly about that kind of work.

People with Realistic interests like practical, physical work with tools, machines, materials, plants or animals.

## Most people are a mix

Few people are only one type. Your Holland Code is usually your top three areas, for example "SAE" (Social, Artistic, Enterprising). Majors that fit two of your top areas are often a better choice than majors that fit only your strongest one. The [College Major Quiz](/college-major-quiz) does this automatically by comparing your whole profile, plus two personality traits (openness and conscientiousness), with every major.

## Then check the practical side

Interests narrow your list. Before you decide, check each finalist on:

1. **Careers and pay.** Look at the careers each major leads to and their BLS median pay.
2. **Training length.** Some careers need a graduate degree or a license after the major.
3. **Flexibility.** Broad majors keep more doors open; specialized majors give a faster, clearer path.
4. **Your strengths.** If the core courses of a major are a struggle, factor that in early.

Our guide on [how to choose a major](/guides/how-to-choose-a-major) walks through these steps.

## Common questions

### What is the easiest way to find my major?

Take the [College Major Quiz](/college-major-quiz), then take the intro course for your top two or three majors in your first year. Your grades and how much you enjoy the work will tell you more than any list.

### What if my top majors are very different?

That is common and usually means you have two strong interest areas. Look for majors that combine them, such as data visualization (Conventional and Investigative) or public health (Social and Investigative), or pair a major with a minor.

### Can I change later?

Yes. About 30% of students who declare a major change it within three years ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2018/2018434.pdf)). See [should I change my major](/guides/should-i-change-my-major).
