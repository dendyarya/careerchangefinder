A Holland Code is a short way to describe the kind of work you are most interested in. It uses six interest types, known together as RIASEC, and summarizes a person or a career with the two or three types that fit best. Career tests, school counselors and the U.S. Department of Labor all use it, because it connects what you enjoy with the careers that involve that kind of work.

This guide explains each type, how to read a three-letter code, and which U.S. careers fall under each one.

## Where the Holland Code comes from

Psychologist John Holland developed the theory in the mid-20th century. His idea was simple: people do best in work environments that match their interests, and both people and jobs can be described with the same six types. The U.S. Department of Labor's O\*NET database rates every occupation on all six types, and its Interest Profiler measures people on the same scale.

## The six RIASEC types

### R: Realistic, "the doers"

Practical, hands-on work with tools, machines, plants, animals or physical materials. Often outdoors or in workshops.

- **Typical careers:** electricians, truck drivers, carpenters, mechanics, farmers.
- **Common strengths:** working with your hands, fixing things, physical coordination.

### I: Investigative, "the thinkers"

Research, analysis and solving problems with science, math or logic.

- **Typical careers:** software developers, pharmacists, scientists, data scientists, engineers.
- **Common strengths:** curiosity, analysis, working with ideas and data.

### A: Artistic, "the creators"

Creating, designing, writing or performing, often with freedom to express ideas.

- **Typical careers:** graphic designers, writers, producers and directors, musicians.
- **Common strengths:** imagination, originality, working without fixed rules.

### S: Social, "the helpers"

Teaching, caring for, advising or serving people.

- **Typical careers:** registered nurses, teachers, counselors, social workers.
- **Common strengths:** empathy, explaining, listening, patience.

### E: Enterprising, "the persuaders"

Leading, selling, starting things and influencing people toward goals.

- **Typical careers:** sales representatives, managers, lawyers, real estate agents.
- **Common strengths:** persuasion, leadership, taking risks.

### C: Conventional, "the organizers"

Working with data, records and clear procedures, where accuracy matters.

- **Typical careers:** accountants, bookkeepers, administrative assistants, customer service representatives.
- **Common strengths:** organization, attention to detail, following and improving processes.

## How U.S. careers divide across the six types

We counted the first letter of the O\*NET Holland Code for each of the 875 careers with full data on this site, and the median pay of those with their own BLS pay figure:

| First letter | Type | Careers | Median of their median pay | Largest careers in the group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | Realistic | 375 | $51,610 | Hand laborers and material movers, janitors, truck drivers |
| C | Conventional | 161 | $51,725 | Fast food workers, cashiers, stockers, customer service |
| S | Social | 118 | $72,300 | Registered nurses, nursing assistants, teachers |
| I | Investigative | 105 | $109,370 | Software developers, systems analysts, nurse practitioners, pharmacists |
| E | Enterprising | 92 | $78,790 | Retail salespersons, general and operations managers |
| A | Artistic | 24 | $74,350 | Graphic designers, producers and directors, editors |

Sources: O\*NET 31.0 (Holland codes); BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 (pay and employment).

Two things stand out. Realistic careers are by far the most numerous, which means people with Realistic interests have the widest choice of jobs. And Investigative careers have the highest median pay, because many of them need advanced degrees.

## How to read a three-letter code

Your code lists your top interest types in order. For example:

- **SAE:** mostly Social, then Artistic, then Enterprising. Think of careers that help people in creative or persuasive ways, such as teaching art or public relations.
- **RIC:** mostly Realistic, then Investigative, then Conventional. Think of technical careers that combine hands-on work with analysis and precision, such as engineering technicians.
- **ECS:** mostly Enterprising, then Conventional, then Social. Chief executives have this code in O\*NET.

The first letter matters most. The second and third narrow the field. Two people with the same first letter but different second letters may enjoy quite different jobs.

## Using your code to change careers

1. **Compare your code with your current job's code.** Each career profile on this site shows its Holland Code. If they differ a lot, that may explain why your work feels wrong.
2. **Look for careers that share your first letter** and use skills you already have. The [career change tool](/career-change/tool) shows how close each option is to your current job.
3. **Do not treat the code as a verdict.** Interests are one part of the picture, along with skills, values, pay and lifestyle.

## Common questions

### What is the rarest Holland Code type?

Among U.S. careers in O\*NET, Artistic is the least common first letter: only 24 of 875 careers on this site start with A. That does not make artistic interests rare in people, but it means fewer jobs are mainly artistic.

### Which Holland Code type pays the most?

In our data, careers whose first letter is Investigative have the highest median pay, $109,370 across 69 careers with their own BLS pay figure. Many of them need a bachelor's or graduate degree.

### Can my Holland Code change?

Interests are fairly stable in adults but can shift over time, especially between your teens and early twenties or after major life changes. Retaking an interest test every few years is reasonable.

### Is the Holland Code the same as a personality test?

No. It measures interests, what kinds of work you enjoy, not personality traits. For personality, see the [Big Five personality test](/big-five-personality-test) and our guide to [Big Five traits and careers](/guides/big-five-personality-traits-and-careers).
