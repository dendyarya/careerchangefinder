A career change can take a few weeks or several years. The difference comes down to one question: how much new preparation does the new career need compared with what you already have? This guide gives realistic timelines for each type of move, using training data for 875 U.S. careers.

## The short answer

- **A move to a close career with the same preparation:** usually 1 to 6 months, mostly job searching and on-the-job training.
- **A move that needs a certificate or license:** usually 6 to 18 months, including a short program.
- **A move that needs a new degree:** usually 2 to 6 years, depending on the degree.

Most career changes fall into the first group. Across all careers on this site, 88.2% of each job's 25 closest career matches usually need no more preparation than the current job.

## Preparation levels: the O\*NET Job Zones

O\*NET, the U.S. Department of Labor's career database, sorts every career into one of five Job Zones based on the education, training and experience it usually needs. The 875 careers with full data on this site fall into four of them:

| Job Zone | What it usually means | Careers | Median of their median pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2: Some preparation | High school diploma, some training | 322 | $47,540 |
| 3: Medium preparation | Vocational training, certificate or associate degree | 194 | $62,370 |
| 4: Considerable preparation | Bachelor's degree | 213 | $85,715 |
| 5: Extensive preparation | Graduate degree | 146 | $98,890 |

Pay: middle value of the BLS May 2025 medians for careers with their own pay figure in each zone.

**The rule of thumb:** a move within the same Job Zone, or to a lower one, usually needs no new schooling. A move up one zone often means a certificate or degree. A move up two zones usually means several years of study.

## How long on-the-job training takes

Even without school, most careers need some training once you are hired. O\*NET asks workers how long it took to learn the job. Here is the most common answer for each of the 875 careers:

| Most common on-the-job training | Careers |
|---|---|
| None or a short demonstration | 66 |
| Up to 1 month | 201 |
| 1 to 3 months | 190 |
| 3 to 6 months | 113 |
| 6 months to 1 year | 188 |
| 1 to 2 years | 56 |
| 2 to 4 years | 36 |
| 4 years or more | 16 |

For more than half of all careers, workers say they learned the job in three months or less. Some careers have no single most common answer in the data, so the counts add up to slightly less than 875.

## Timelines by type of move

### Same preparation, close skills: 1 to 6 months

Examples: a cashier moving to bank teller, a customer service representative moving to sales, an office clerk moving to title examination.

What takes the time:

- 2 to 8 weeks to research options and update your resume;
- 1 to 3 months of applications and interviews;
- a few weeks to a few months of on-the-job training once hired.

You can often make this move while still working your current job.

### Certificate or license: 6 to 18 months

Examples: moving into medical records, real estate sales, commercial driving, phlebotomy or HVAC work.

What takes the time:

- a program of a few weeks to a year, often in the evening or online;
- an exam or license application;
- a job search, often helped by the program's employer contacts.

### Associate degree: about 2 years

Examples: dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy, physical therapist assistant work. Many associate programs are built for adults and run part time, which can stretch them to three years.

### Bachelor's or graduate degree: 4 years or more

Examples: becoming a registered nurse without prior college, an engineer, a lawyer or a physician. If you already hold a bachelor's degree, accelerated programs can shorten some paths, such as second-degree nursing programs.

## 5 ways to shorten your timeline

1. **Choose a close match.** The more skills you already have, the less you need to learn. Start with the [career change tool](/career-change/tool).
2. **Check whether credit counts.** Colleges and training programs often give credit for past courses, military training or work experience.
3. **Learn while you work.** Evening, online and employer-paid programs keep income coming in while you train.
4. **Use a bridge job.** A job next to the target career, such as a hospital unit clerk before nursing, gives you experience and contacts during training.
5. **Apply before you finish.** For many certificate programs, employers hire students in their last months.

## What slows a career change down

- **Choosing a distant career first.** A move that needs two higher Job Zones adds years.
- **Too many options.** People who keep comparing ten careers for a year often never start. Narrow to two or three and test them.
- **Waiting for the perfect time.** A small first step, such as one course, makes the decision clearer.
- **Skipping the money plan.** If you run out of savings halfway through a program, the change stops. Plan the budget before you begin.

## A sample 6-month plan for a close move

| Month | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose | List the 25 closest careers in the [career change tool](/career-change/tool), shortlist three, read their profiles |
| 2 | Test | Talk to two people in each career, read 15 job ads, take one short course |
| 3 | Decide and prepare | Pick one career, close the main skill gap, rewrite your resume and LinkedIn |
| 4 | Apply | Send 5 to 10 targeted applications a week, ask contacts for referrals |
| 5 | Interview | Practice your answer to "Why the change?", negotiate offers |
| 6 | Start | Begin on-the-job training while your experience is still fresh |

For a move that needs a certificate, add the program length between months 3 and 4. For a degree, plan the switch in two stages: first a close move that keeps your income, then the degree part time.

## Common questions

### How long does it take to change careers at 40?

The same as at any age: a few months for a close move with the same preparation, about a year with a certificate, and several years with a new degree. See [career change at 40](/career-change/at-40) for options that fit each timeline.

### What is the fastest career to switch to?

A career close to your current one, in the same or a lower Job Zone, with short on-the-job training. For more than half of all careers, workers learned the job in three months or less.

### Can I change careers in 6 months?

Often, yes, if you choose a close match that needs no new degree. Use the [career change tool](/career-change/tool) and look for matches that need no more preparation.

### Do I need to go back to school?

For most moves, no. Read [should you go back to school to change careers?](/guides/go-back-to-school-to-change-careers) to decide when school is worth it.
