Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October 2026, exactly matching the rise in average hourly pay, so workers' real hourly earnings did not grow at all, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Tuesday, November 10.

The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) increased 0.4% on a seasonally adjusted basis in October, after rising 0.6% in September. Prices excluding food and energy, often called core inflation, rose 0.3% for the third month in a row.

*Seasonally adjusted monthly changes from the BLS Consumer Price Index release.*

Grocery prices (food at home) rose 0.4% and restaurant prices (food away from home) rose 0.5%. The motor vehicle insurance index rose 0.7%.

The usual year-over-year comparison is missing this month. BLS did not collect survey data for October 2025 because of a lapse in appropriations, so it cannot publish 12-month changes for most October 2026 indexes.

## Paychecks only kept pace

The companion Real Earnings release showed average hourly earnings for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls rose 0.4% in October, to $38.00. Because prices rose by the same amount, real average hourly earnings were unchanged from September, and real average weekly earnings were also unchanged.

For production and nonsupervisory employees, real average hourly earnings rose 0.1%, but real average weekly earnings fell 0.3% because the average workweek got shorter.

## What it means for career changers

When raises only match price increases, the main way to get ahead is a higher-paying job, not a bigger raise in the same one. Many careers that pay well above typical earnings need training after high school but not a four-year degree. O*NET places these in Job Zone 3, where most jobs call for vocational training, related experience or an associate degree.
